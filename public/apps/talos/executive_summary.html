Overview · Executive summary

Executive summary

Can a Greek/EU team build and operate a 100–150 kg passive-RF satellite that grows into a geolocating constellation, and should it? Yes — with four conditions that are commercial and regulatory rather than physical. This page states the conclusion, the evidence behind it and the decisions it asks for.

Issue 0.3 · 21 October 2026Evidence: five research notes, 220 sources checked October 2026

Bottom line

The mission is technically feasible with today's catalogue parts and a modest amount of custom RF work. The physics favours it: ground radars are visible from low Earth orbit through their sidelobes from anywhere in the footprint, tactical UHF/L-band radios sit 15–25 dB above the receiver noise at 1,500 km, and the satellite's own position and clock can be tied to GNSS at the metre and 20-nanosecond level that time-difference geolocation needs. The market has been proven by HawkEye 360 ($117.7 M revenue in 2025, profitable, IPO at $2.4 B in May 2026) and the 150 kg class has just been validated by Unseenlabs' Gen-2 BRO-31 (L/S/C/X/Ku) and BAE Systems' Azalea trio. Europe has no sovereign commercial supplier of this data outside France, and the EU's own agencies (EUSPA, EDF) have issued demand signals for space-based GNSS-interference monitoring and persistent ISR.

The hard parts are not detection but dynamic range (a surveillance radar's mainlobe arrives at −24 dBm next to −120 dBm communications), self-interference on a small bus, timing for TDOA/FDOA, and the data reduction that turns gigabits per second of IQ into kilobytes of reports before the next ground contact. All four have known engineering answers and all four are retired by a pathfinder flight before the 150 kg satellite is committed.

Recommendation

Proceed to Phase A on the TALOS baseline (530 km / 53°, dual-path UHF–X receiver, 4-element DF interferometer, GNSS-disciplined timing, 1 Gbit/s X-band downlink), run a 16U pathfinder in parallel with Phase B, and do not launch the demonstrator until funding for the first three-satellite cluster is committed. The cluster, not the demonstrator, is the product.

The concept in five sentences

TALOS-1 is an ESPA-class satellite at 530 km and 53° inclination — the orbit that maximises passes over the Eastern Mediterranean, Aegean, Black Sea and Middle East — carrying a dual-path receiver: a high-dynamic-range tuner path for narrowband communications from 70 MHz to 6 GHz and a direct-sampling RFSoC path that digitises 2 GHz blocks of S-, C- and X-band for radars. Its antennas are deployable UHF helices, hemispheric wideband survey elements, S- and X-band patch arrays and a four-element 1–12 GHz interferometer on a 1.2 m cross-boom that gives a line of bearing from a single snapshot. On board, every radar pulse becomes a descriptor word, every communications burst a signal report, and only prioritised IQ snapshots — 20 kB to 5 MB each — are stored for downlink, so that a day's collection fits in the passes over Greece. Three such satellites flying 150 km apart turn those time-stamped snapshots into instantaneous TDOA/FDOA fixes; three clusters in three planes turn fixes into tracks and patterns of life; twelve or more give sub-hour regional revisit. The products are detections, geolocations with confidence ellipses, an emitter catalogue with maturity levels, and alerts on change — delivered as GeoJSON/OGC APIs and NATO STANAG 4676 tracks from a sovereign EU enclave.

What it will and will not do

Will

  • Detect and fingerprint naval, coastal, air-defence and AEW&C radars from anywhere in a 2,000 km footprint; catalogue them by PRI, pulse width, scan and modulation-on-pulse.
  • Map military-class GNSS jammers and EW systems, tactical UHF/L nets, TETRA, UHF SATCOM, L-band satphones, military UAS ground stations.
  • Geolocate persistent emitters to 1–5 km with one satellite over several passes and to hundreds of metres to a few kilometres instantly with a cluster.
  • Cue SAR/EO and maritime assets; correlate RF with AIS/ADS-B to flag dark vessels and spoofing.
  • Find a satellite operator's uplink interferer — the NOAA GOES case shows a 1.5 kHz emitter is findable with persistence.

Will not

  • Detect consumer drones, 10 mW personal GNSS jammers or hand-held links at 2.4/5.8 GHz from 500 km — they are 10–45 dB below the noise.
  • Intercept content. The system measures externals (frequency, time, bandwidth, modulation, power, position). Content is a separately governed state mode, not a commercial product.
  • Give tactical-latency alerts in Stage 1: store-and-forward over a Greek-latitude ground network means 1–10 hours; minutes need polar/regional stations or an inter-satellite link.
  • Out-scale HawkEye 360. It differentiates on sovereignty, regional persistence, X-band coverage and fusion with national assets, not on satellite count.

Top risks

  1. Funding gap between demonstrator and cluster (P-01, score 20) — the Kleos failure mode. Mitigation: commit both before launch; milestone-paid anchor.
  2. Self-interference / EMC (T-02, 16) — transmitters, converters and clocks 100 dB above the signals of interest. Mitigation: EMC budget at Phase A, EM-level RF compatibility test, filtered payload power, opposite-face placement, transmit blanking.
  3. Launch delay or failure (T-12, 16) — two launch options, orbit-agnostic design, launch insurance for the FM.
  4. Customer concentration (P-02, 16) — NATO/EU agencies, allied MoDs, regulators and maritime insurers alongside the anchor.
  5. Cyber (T-17, 15) — a SIGINT constellation is a target; zero-trust ground segment, HSM-held keys, NIS2 compliance from day one.

Timing sync, SEU/latch-up of the commercial RFSoC, the downlink bottleneck, export licensing and radiation lifetime all score 12 and are the risks a pathfinder flight retires. The full register is in section 02.

Decisions requested

  1. Approve Phase 0/A (6–9 months, 6–10 FTE) on the TALOS baseline, with the legal opinion, licensing path and export classification as its first deliverables (gate G0).
  2. Approve the pathfinder strategy: a 16U single-chain pathfinder in parallel with Phase B (€5–9 M), or a hosted payload (€1.5–4 M) if the budget forbids both.
  3. Set the funding rule: no demonstrator launch without committed cluster funding (≥ €25–35 M incremental).
  4. Start the anchor-customer conversation now: Hellenic MoD / HNDGS, EUSPA (GNSS-RFI service), EDF consortium, NATO APSS.
  5. Adopt ITAR-free as a design rule, with EAR-only semiconductors as the accepted residual.
