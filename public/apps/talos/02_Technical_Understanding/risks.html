02 Technical understanding · Risks

Risk register

Thirty risks, scored likelihood × impact on 1–5 scales, from the programme's own analysis of what has killed or crippled comparable ventures. The register is dominated by five things: self-interference on a small bus, the launch manifest, a cyber-attack on a SIGINT ground segment, the funding cliff between demonstrator and cluster that ended Kleos, and dependence on a single government customer. Most of the technical risks can be retired before the 150 kg flight model is committed — which is the argument for the 16U pathfinder.

The ten that dominate

Ranked by score; where scores tie, the risk with the weaker mitigation ranks higher. The narrative says why each sits where it does and what would move it.

    Why these dominate

    Self-interference (T-02) is the only 16-point technical risk because it is near-certain to appear and expensive to fix late: every small-satellite RF payload has found its own bus in the data, and the UHF band — where the DC/DC converter harmonics live — is where TALOS looks for tactical radios 100 dB below the bus noise. It is mitigated by design (filtered payload PCDU, opposite-face placement, blanking) but only retired by a test: the EM-level bus-plus-payload RF compatibility test in Phase C and the on-orbit interference fingerprint.

    Launch (T-12) scores 16 because rideshare manifests slip routinely and a slip of six months on a 42-month programme consumes the schedule float and part of the funding runway — Kleos' months-long launch delay is what first made its liquidity "tenuous"[R5-5]. Two booked options and an orbit-agnostic design (53° or SSO) are the mitigations; launch insurance covers the loss case.

    Cyber (T-17) is a 15 on impact alone: a SIGINT constellation operated from Greece is a target of nation-state interest from the first day it has customers, and a successful intrusion is simultaneously loss of control, loss of the data product and a licence breach under NIS2 and the EU Space Act[R5-12]. Zero-trust ground architecture, AES-256 authenticated telecommand with HSM key management and a separately accredited classified enclave are not optional extras.

    The funding gap (P-01) is the single highest score in the register, 20, because it is exactly how Kleos died: four clusters in orbit, €272 k of revenue, a lender that pulled the plug in July 2023[R5-2][R5-3]. The mitigation is a programme rule, not a technology: do not launch the demonstrator until the cluster is funded, and anchor it on a government contract that pays on milestones. Customer concentration (P-02) and competition at scale (P-03) are the other side of the same coin: HawkEye 360 has 40+ satellites, a $285 M backlog and a public listing[R4-2]; a European entrant survives on sovereign positioning, regional persistence and a customer base wider than one ministry.

    Timing (T-03), radiation (T-04, T-18), downlink (T-05), geolocation accuracy (T-07), supplier exit (T-13) and export control (T-14) all score 12. They share a property: each is testable or decidable early. Timing and radiation are what a pathfinder flight measures; downlink is arithmetic the data budget already closes; export classification is a Phase A legal task; supplier fragility is a procurement clause (SFL, builder of HawkEye's first clusters, stopped manufacturing[R5-27]).

    Risk retirement plan

    A risk is "retired" when evidence — a test, a flight, a signed document — moves its likelihood to 1 or its impact below mission-affecting. The plan below maps each technical risk to the earliest event that retires it, which is the schedule logic behind running the 16U pathfinder in parallel with Phase B rather than after it.

    What the pathfinder buys

    Flying one UHF/L/S chain with the RFSoC, the timing unit and the firmware on a 16U bus in 2029 retires or reduces before the flight-model CDR — the risks whose mitigations are all "characterise in orbit". At €5–9 M (est.) that is 20–25 % of the demonstrator cost spent to de-risk the other 75 %. The hosted-payload alternative retires the radiation and firmware items but not the antenna, EMC-on-our-own-bus or licensing-precedent items. See pathfinder options.

    How the register is run

    • Ownership. Every risk has one named owner (a role in the programme); owners re-score at each review (SRR, PDR, CDR, QR) and the register is a standing agenda item of the monthly programme review.
    • Scoring. Likelihood 1 (rare) to 5 (almost certain) over the programme life; impact 1 (negligible) to 5 (mission or company loss). Score = L × I; ≥ 12 red requires an active mitigation with a date and budget line, 8–11 amber requires a monitoring trigger, < 8 green is accepted.
    • Contingency link. The 20–30 % Phase A contingency in the cost model is allocated against red risks first; a risk that is retired releases its share.
    • Kill criteria. Four conditions stop the programme regardless of score: no lawful basis for the collection model, a licence refusal or ITAR classification that closes EU-controlled sales, a pathfinder RFSoC lifetime under 18 months without a credible fix, and failure to secure cluster funding before the demonstrator launch.
