02 Technical understanding · Bus

Bus subsystems

The TALOS bus is deliberately a bought, ESPA-class European platform — but a SIGINT payload makes demands that an imaging or comms bus does not meet off the shelf: a UHF-quiet power system, star-tracker attitude knowledge for direction finding, a nanosecond-class timing interface, two switchable 150 W payload lines, transmitters on the face opposite the receive antennas, and enough propulsion to hold a 150 km formation and dispose of the spacecraft within five years. This page states each requirement, the baseline choice from the component model, the European (ITAR-free) supplier field for it, and the sizing behind the numbers.

What a SIGINT payload asks of its bus

Research note 02 sets the envelope: the bus must carry ≥ 40 kg of payload, deliver ≥ 150 W orbit-average to it, downlink ≥ 500 Mbit/s and point to ~0.05° for the directional antennas. The TALOS component model lands inside that envelope (payload kg core, W collecting), so mass and power are not the hard part. The hard part is the list below — requirements that are specific to a −120 dBm receiver sharing a 0.6 m box with its own transmitters and switching converters[R3-2].

Baseline bus decision and what to negotiate

Baseline: an EU ESPA-class bus bought fixed-price with the RF-quietness, timing-interface and transmitter-placement requirements written into the specification, under a frame agreement covering pathfinder, demonstrator and cluster options. The MP42H is the most mature EU candidate but is payload-limited at 35 kg and ~100 W in its catalogue form — the Gen-2 MP42 (40–50 kg payload, 150 kg launch mass) or a stretched variant must be negotiated[R5-16]. Aerospacelab VSP and BST LEOS-100 offer more power margin; Triton-X and the EnduroSat microsat need flight-status verification at contract time. Supplier fragility (T-13) argues for escrow of design data whichever vendor wins.

Propulsion and the ΔV budget

Propulsion is optional for the demonstrator's core mission and mandatory for everything after it: holding the 3-satellite formation to ±5 km for three years, making up drag at 530 km, avoiding conjunctions in a congested shell, and lowering the orbit to ≤ 350 km at end of life so that re-entry happens inside the five-year rule[R3-21][R3-22]. HawkEye's published formation budget — ≈ 35/29/5 m/s per spacecraft of 100 m/s available over three years — is the open benchmark for the formation line[R3-2].

ADCS sizing note

The attitude control problem for TALOS is modest in torque and demanding in knowledge. The deployed spacecraft (wings, helices, boom) has an inertia of order 15–25 kg·m² about the pitch and roll axes (est.), the disturbance torques below are micro-newton-metre class, and the wheels only have to hold nadir pointing within 0.05° and execute an occasional slew for a downlink pass or a cued reflector dwell. The calculator applies the standard first-order disturbance models (gravity gradient, aerodynamic, solar pressure, residual magnetic dipole) to the TALOS geometry and compares the resulting momentum build-up with the 0.5 N·m·s wheels in the baseline.

Models: gravity gradient \(T_g = \tfrac{3\mu}{2R^3}\,|I_z - I_y|\sin 2\theta\); aerodynamic \(T_a = \tfrac12\rho C_d A v^2\,\Delta\), with \(\rho\) from an exponential fit (≈ 2 × 10⁻¹³ kg/m³ at 530 km, mean solar activity, est.); solar \(T_s = \tfrac{F_s}{c} A (1+q)\,\Delta\) with q = 0.6; magnetic \(T_m = D\,B\) with \(B \approx 2 B_0 (R_E/R)^3\) at high latitude. Treating all torques as secular over an orbit is conservative; in practice gravity-gradient and solar torques are largely cyclic. Magnetorquers dump the accumulated momentum outside collection windows — the EMC rule that ties ADCS to the payload.

Two conclusions. First, the residual magnetic dipole is the term that matters at this size — keep the battery harness twisted and the wheel-driver loops small, and specify ≤ 0.5 A·m² at acceptance. Second, with 0.5 N·m·s wheels in a pyramid the spacecraft dumps momentum every few orbits with magnetorquers, which is routine, but the dumps must be scheduled in the gaps between collection dwells because magnetorquer PWM is a UHF interferer. The star trackers (Arcsec Sagitta class, 2 arcsec) are the knowledge source; the STIM300 IMU propagates between star-tracker updates and through sun/Earth exclusion. Everything in this chain is available ITAR-free in Europe at a complete-ADCS budget of about €250–450 k (est.).

Thermal and harness

Thermal. The bus thermal design is conventional — MLI, ±X radiators, survival heaters on battery and propulsion — with one twist: the payload has its own thermally controlled plate and radiator, and the bus heater cycling must not be allowed to modulate the DF front-end temperature. The 53° orbit has beta-angle seasons that swing from full-sun to 36-minute eclipses; the SSO alternative is thermally benign but changes the sun angle on the wings. Both cases are closed in the power budget. The RFSoC's ~48 W in a 0.2 × 0.16 m housing is the hot spot (risk T-08), carried out by a variable-conductance heat pipe to the −X radiator (0.25 m²).

Harness. Grounding and routing are half of the EMC solution. The baseline harness uses twisted-shielded pairs, a single-point ground, separate payload and bus returns, and keeps every power line away from the UHF front-end; the payload's own phase-matched RF harness (2.2 kg, phase-stable coax matched to ±2 mm) is accounted separately in the payload budget because it is routinely under-estimated on first payloads.

Decisions carried into the baseline

  • Buy the bus (EU, ESPA-class, fixed price) with the SIGINT-specific requirements above in the specification; build the payload. Frame agreement across pathfinder, demonstrator and cluster.
  • Star-tracker ADCS (2 × Sagitta class), 4 × 0.5 N·m·s wheels in a pyramid, STIM300 IMU, magnetorquers scheduled outside collection windows.
  • 28 V unregulated bus with MPPT and two switchable 150 W payload lines; 620 W BOL / 540 W EOL deployable wings; 420 Wh battery at ≤ 30 % DoD.
  • FEEP electric propulsion, 170 m/s at 150 kg: 60 formation, 40 drag, 50 disposal, 20 margin.
  • X-band 1 Gbit/s on +X, S-band TT&C with AES-256 authentication, X-band receive blanking and TT&C time-gating as flight rules.
  • Payload timing chain independent of the bus; bus provides PVT and attitude with timestamps.
