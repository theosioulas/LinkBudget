02 Technical understanding · RF chain & digitiser

RF chain & digitiser

Twelve receive chains in two families: four high-dynamic-range tuner channels for UHF and L-band, eight block-downconverted S/C/X chains into a 5 GSPS RFSoC. This page sets the gain and noise budget of a chain (with a cascade calculator), the frequency plan with the notches for the satellite's own transmitters, the ADC dynamic-range arithmetic, the LO stability needed for FDOA, and the digitiser board candidates with their export status. The chain's job is simple to state and hard to do: keep a 2.5 dB noise figure while surviving −24 dBm pulses.

Baseline: TALOS-1 v0.3Sources: research notes 02 §C–D, 03 §4

One chain, stage by stage

Every chain follows the same order (research 03 §4.3): a limiter that protects the LNA from the strongest pulses, a pre-select filter that removes out-of-band energy before any gain, the LNA that sets the noise figure, a step attenuator that extends the strong-signal range under microsecond AGC control, a block converter or tuner, an anti-alias filter and the ADC. The diagram is drawn from the calculator below: edit a stage and the running noise figure and gain update. Rule of thumb from the research: total gain is set so the thermal floor sits 10–15 dB above the ADC's quantisation floor, leaving 60–70 dB of headroom to full scale on the direct-sampling path and ≈ 80 dB on the tuner path; the attenuator buys another 30 dB in microseconds.

Cascade noise-figure and gain calculator

Friis' formula for N stages with linear gains \(G_i\) and noise factors \(F_i\):

$$ F_{sys} = F_1 + \frac{F_2 - 1}{G_1} + \frac{F_3 - 1}{G_1 G_2} + \cdots, \qquad NF_{sys} = 10\log_{10} F_{sys} $$

Edit gains and noise figures (a passive stage has NF equal to its loss). The presets reproduce the three TALOS chain families as specified in components.js. Headroom is computed against the ADC full-scale input you set; the RFSoC and the 16-bit tuner ADC have different full-scale levels and ENOB, so the presets switch those too.

Frequency plan and filter banks

Block down-conversion uses a shared, GNSS-disciplined 100 MHz OCXO reference (research 03 §4.2). The S/C/X bank mixes each 2 GHz block into the RFSoC's first Nyquist zone (0–2 GHz at 5 GSPS); the L-band and UHF chains feed the tuner, which is agile over 200 MHz windows. Two notches are mandatory: the S-band TT&C transmitter at 2200–2290 MHz sits inside the 2–2.5 GHz block, and the X-band downlink at 8025–8400 MHz sits inside the 8–10 GHz block, 200 MHz from the X-band radar window. Both are handled by filtering plus time gating — receive is blanked while the transmitter is keyed.

With eight RFSoC chains and five 2 GHz blocks, not every block is digitised on every chain at once. The S/C/X bank's switched pre-selectors let the payload controller assign blocks to chains per task: for example the four DF chains on the 2–4 GHz block for an AEW&C search while the S-array H/V pair stays on 2.5–3.0 GHz and the X-array pair on 8–10 GHz. Whole-band staring across 2–12 GHz would need 5 × 8 = 40 ADC channels; the alternative — time-multiplexing blocks with dwell times set by the scan periods of the expected radars (seconds) — is what ESM receivers have always done.

Direct sampling versus superheterodyne

The architecture decision is taken on the architecture page (D-01); here is the RF-level consequence. Direct sampling at 5 GSPS puts the whole 2 GHz block into one converter with ENOB ≈ 9–10 (56–62 dB over Nyquist); its strength is bandwidth, time-of-arrival resolution (≈ 1/fs with interpolation to ~1 ns) and eight phase-coherent channels on one die for interferometry[R3-19]. The tuner path trades bandwidth for cleanliness: analogue pre-selection ahead of a 16-bit, 250 MSPS converter with ENOB 12–13 gives 75–80 dB of range, exactly what 25 kHz comms next to 60 dBm cellular base stations need. HawkEye's history is the precedent for both: AD9361-class tuners on Pathfinder[R3-1], Xiphos Q-series RFSoC processors on Clusters 8–9 and after[R2-x84].

ADC dynamic range and processing gain

The converter's signal-to-noise ratio over its Nyquist band and the gain from looking in a narrower bin:

$$ SNR_{Nyq} \approx 6.02\,ENOB + 1.76\ \text{dB}, \qquad G_{proc} = 10\log_{10}\frac{f_s/2}{B_{bin}}, \qquad DR_{bin} \approx SNR_{Nyq} + G_{proc} $$

At 5 GSPS with ENOB 9.5 the Nyquist SNR is ≈ 59 dB; a 1 MHz bin adds 34 dB, so ≈ 93 dB of range per bin — close to the 95 dB target in baseline.js — provided the converter is never clipped. Clipping is a global event: intermodulation from one saturating pulse lands in every bin of the block. That is why the step attenuator and the limiter are in the chain and why the over-range flag drives blanking in the comms channels. The small calculator makes the trade visible: the tuner's 16-bit converter wins at the Nyquist level, the RFSoC wins per bin because of its sample rate, and the two end up within a few dB of each other for narrow bins.

Reference, LO and phase noise for FDOA

Every frequency the payload measures is referenced to the 100 MHz GNSS-disciplined OCXO in the timing unit; the LO synthesiser multiplies it to 2/4/6/8/10 GHz for the block converters. FDOA across two satellites needs the difference of the two received frequencies to be known to a fraction of the thermal FDOA error: for the 25 kHz / 100 ms / 10 dB case the thermal σf is ≈ 0.035 Hz, i.e. ≈ 14 m on the ground, and the ground gradient is ≈ 400 m per Hz (research 03 §6.2). So the LOs of two satellites must agree to ≪ 0.1 Hz at 1 GHz — a relative stability of 10−10 over the snapshot. GNSS-disciplined OCXOs hold 10−11–10−12 short-term; free-running TCXOs (10−7) are two to three orders short, which is why the timing unit is a never-off item with CSAC holdover. The baseline requirement in baseline.js is short-term.

Multiplication penalty: the 10 GHz LO for the top X block is 100 × the reference, so reference phase noise degrades by 40 dB. A −150 dBc/Hz reference at 10 kHz (space OCXO class, Rakon / AXTAL[R2-x87]) becomes −110 dBc/Hz at 10 GHz before the PLL's own contribution, which is the specification line in components.js for the LO unit. This is also why the X-band chains carry 1.8 dB of noise figure rather than the 1.2 dB of S/C: the first-stage LNA at 10 GHz is simply noisier.

EMC, self-interference and thermal rules

These are the design rules the chain imposes on the rest of the spacecraft; they are derived in research 03 §4.5 and expanded on the environment page.

EMC and self-interference

  • Receive blanking during S-band TT&C and X-band downlink transmission; the payload controller holds the transmit schedule.
  • > 80 dB isolation to the X-band receive path: notch filter at 8.0–8.4 GHz in the X-array feed plus the array on the face opposite the downlink antenna.
  • Conducted-emission filtering on every payload rail to 40 dB below MIL-STD-461 CE102 in 225–512 MHz; converters synchronised so harmonics avoid the UHF pre-select passband.
  • Shielded enclosures for all front-ends; single-point grounding; payload and bus returns separated.
  • A self-test mode that records the spacecraft's own interference "fingerprint" at commissioning (all bus units cycling) so it can be masked or subtracted.
  • Magnetorquer PWM and wheel speed changes scheduled outside UHF collection windows.

Thermal

  • LNA gain drifts ≈ 0.01 dB/°C and phase ≈ 0.5–1°/°C per stage est.; four DF chains on one temperature-controlled plate at 20 ± 2 °C keeps the inter-chain differential below the 5° phase budget between calibrations.
  • RFSoC junction ≤ 85 °C with 48 W dissipated: variable-conductance heat pipe to the −X radiator (0.25 m²), separate from the bus radiators.
  • Phase-stable coax (±0.5°/m over −20…+50 °C at 3 GHz) matched to ±2 mm between the DF elements and the bank.
  • Calibration tone injected at every element every orbit removes the residual gain and phase drift.
  • Collection duty cycle over the SAA and during thruster arcs is a thermal as well as a power decision (see environment).

Candidate digitiser boards

The digitiser is the architectural fork identified in the supplier survey: a Zynq RFSoC COTS board (or a custom board around a ZU47DR/ZU48DR) versus a Teledyne EV12AQ600 + PolarFire RT design that is more radiation-tolerant and EU-sourced for the ADC but needs far more custom work. Export notes are copied from the supplier catalogue; all RFSoC parts are US EAR (3A001/4A994) and the high-rate ADC thresholds also trigger EU dual-use 3A001.a.5, so even the "EU" ADC needs licences for non-EU end use[R2-x79].

The Xiphos precedent de-risks the RFSoC choice; a custom EU carrier board around the ZU48DR is preferred over the Q8RF because the 14-bit, 5 GSPS converters match the 2 GHz block plan and keep the X-band chains on the same die as the DF chains. The decision is revisited at PDR against two facts: whether the US export licence for the RFSoC and the ADI mixers is granted with acceptable re-export conditions, and whether radiation lot tests (ECSS-Q-ST-60-13 style) of the chosen die show SEL thresholds compatible with the latch-up protection design.
