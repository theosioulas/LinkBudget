How to read the model

Decks, not drawers

The six decks are a conceptual stacking in the direction Earth-facing (bottom) to zenith (top). The real spacecraft is an ESPA-class box of roughly 0.6 × 0.6 × 0.8 m; the point of the stack is to show which units sit on the same thermally controlled plate (the RF deck), which share the radiator (the digital deck), and why transmitters and receivers live on opposite faces.

Links carry the design rules

RF links (orange) are phase-matched coax where they feed the DF interferometer. Timing links (green) all originate in one unit — that is the TDOA/FDOA budget made visible. Power links (yellow) pass through the filtered payload PCDU because the UHF band is a victim of switching converters. Data links (blue) converge on the payload controller, the one unit that must never crash.

Optional items

Dashed units (0.7 m reflector, ML co-processor, inter-satellite link) are not in the TALOS-1 core budget. Toggle them off to see the demonstrator as costed; leave them on to see the Stage 2 cluster configuration.

Where the numbers come from

Masses and powers are allocations from the research notes (payload 25–45 kg, 90–160 W collecting; bus elements from the supplier catalogue), reconciled so that the component list, the budgets page and the baseline agree. They are inputs to Phase A, not measured values.
