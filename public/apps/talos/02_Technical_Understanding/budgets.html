02 Technical understanding · Budgets

Mass, power, link & data budgets

Every number on this page is computed at load time from the component model and the mission baseline, so a change in components.js or baseline.js flows through here. The verdict: TALOS-1 closes with room to spare — ≈ 87 kg dry core (104 kg with 20 % system margin) inside a 150 kg allocation, a 620 W array against a ≈ 195 W orbit-average demand, an X-band link that supports the full 1 Gbit/s at mid elevations into a 3.7 m dish, and 200+ GB/day of downlink capacity against ≈ 30 GB/day of product. The tight budgets are not mass and power: they are the 20 ns timing allocation and the margins that depend on where the ground stations are.

The component model carries items in ten subsystems; three are growth options (deployable reflector, ML co-processor, inter-satellite link) that are not in the TALOS-1 core. Power budget

Power is budgeted by operating mode. The payload figure is the sum of component powers in collection mode; the bus housekeeping figure in the baseline ( W) is higher than the sum of bus components ( W) because it reserves 10–20 W for heaters in eclipse and converter losses. Downlink (X-band transmitter, W) and thrusting (FEEP PPU, W) are added when those loads are on.

Generation = array power × (1 − eclipse fraction) × efficiency, with array power from BASELINE.power (BOL W, EOL W; the slider default is EOL). The SSO line is the noon/midnight case; a 10:30 LTAN sits at ~35 % and a dawn-dusk orbit is nearly eclipse-free. Battery DoD = eclipse duration × eclipse load / Wh.

Link budgets

X-band downlink

The data exit is a Gbit/s DVB-S2 / CCSDS 131.2 X-band link in the 8025–8400 MHz EESS allocation into KSAT-class 3.7 m or 7.3 m dishes[R3-27][R4-33]. Because free-space loss changes by 12 dB between 5° and 90° elevation, DVB-S2 adaptive coding and modulation is what makes the link efficient: a low-order modcod at the horizon, a high-order one near zenith. The table picks, for each elevation, the highest modcod whose required Es/N0 is met with the chosen margin and reports the resulting information rate.

Data budget

The payload produces reports and prioritised IQ snapshots, not raw IQ: four coherent channels at 100 MHz would be 9.6 Gbit/s, a hundred times the downlink. What leaves the satellite is sized from the snapshot plan in the processing chapter (on-board processing & data): per orbit, up to comms snapshots of 20 kB and radar snapshots of 1 MB plus emitter and signal reports, which the baseline rounds to GB/day. What can be taken away depends on where the ground stations are and on the orbit; the table computes a day of passes for each station with ORB.siteStats for the TALOS 53° orbit and the SSO alternative, at the DVB-S2 rates from the link budget above.

Timing budget

The constellation geolocation product needs the time stamps of two satellites to agree to ns (1σ) — the figure HawkEye assumed in its cluster design[R3-2] and the one the thermal-noise terms of the geolocation chapter are compared against. The allocation below splits it between the contributors in the timing chain; terms add in root-sum-square because they are independent. Values are design allocations (est.) to be verified in the pathfinder and in commissioning against known emitters (risk T-03).
