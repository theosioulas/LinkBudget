Roadmap, cost & schedule

What it costs, how long it takes and in what order the money is committed. The cost model triangulates from government fixed-price awards, ESA's €35 M Scout cap, the capital-raised-per-satellite of the operators that actually did this, and the few published list prices. It lands at €18–35 M for the TALOS-1 demonstrator, €40–65 M cumulative for the three-satellite cluster and €110–170 M for twelve satellites over seven to nine years — with the funding gap between demonstrator and cluster, not the technology, as the thing most likely to end the programme.

The scenario totals are consistent with these anchors: a credible first operational SIGINT constellation of ~12 ESPA-class satellites is a €100–170 M, seven-to-nine-year undertaking before commercial G&A. Companies that raised less than ~€100 M before reaching a revenue-generating constellation failed (Kleos)[R5-2]; those that survived raised €120 M (Unseenlabs)[R5-11] to $500 M+ (HawkEye 360, ten years and a May 2026 IPO to reach a ~$200 M revenue run-rate)[R5-6][R5-7].

Schedule

ECSS phasing, tailored. Realistic comparators from founding to first launch: HawkEye 360 39 months, Kleos ~42 months after a launch slip that strained liquidity, Unseenlabs ~48 months[R5-9][R5-5][R5-11]; the Greek national programme took from March 2019 to CDR in December 2024[R5-14], a reminder that institutional programmes run slower than start-ups. Plan the demonstrator for 42 months with a 6-month float.

Roadmap

    Team and headcount

    Payload RF, DSP/FPGA, flight software, systems and mission analysis, product assurance, AIT, operations and flight dynamics, ground/cloud and cyber, RF/ELINT analysts, programme management and procurement, sales and government relations, legal/export/licensing. HawkEye 360 reached a few hundred staff at 39 satellites and Unseenlabs ~100–150 at eleven (est.).

    Recommendation: 16U pathfinder in parallel with Phase B

    Run the 16U pathfinder with one UHF/L/S chain in parallel with Phase B of the 150 kg satellite — not sequentially — so that its flight results feed the demonstrator CDR. This mirrors HawkEye 360's 13 kg Pathfinder → larger Cluster 2 path[R5-9][R5-10] and costs about 20–25 % of the demonstrator. It retires the radiation, timing, EMC and downlink risks (T-02/03/04/05/18) and establishes the Greek licensing precedent before the flight model is committed. If budget forbids both, the hosted-payload route on a GNTS-class national mission is the cheapest way to retire radiation, firmware and timing risk.

    Funding sources

    European defence, security and resilience start-ups raised a record $8.7 B in 2025, four times the 2020 level, driven by late-stage rounds[R5-23]; Greece's HCDI now runs investor-access programmes and Greece ranks highly in EDF participation[R5-24]. The table maps the instruments to the phases they can fund. Positioning as dual-use (maritime domain awareness, GNSS-interference monitoring, spectrum support for EETT, SAR cueing) widens the base and eases export; the defence-grade products are the margin.

    Funding rule

    Secure funding to the cluster, not just the demonstrator, before launching the demonstrator; sign anchor-customer contracts that pay on milestones during development; avoid single-lender debt before revenue. Gate G0 requires a funding plan covering the demonstrator and 50 % of the cluster; G2 requires cumulative funding ≥ the demonstrator total; G4 requires €25–35 M incremental secured.
