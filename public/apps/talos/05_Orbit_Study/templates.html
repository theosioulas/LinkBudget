How to read the table

Gaps are in hours (h) or minutes. "Typical" is the median gap between consecutive looks — what an analyst experiences most of the time; "worst" is the longest hole in three days — what a service-level agreement must survive. A dash means the latitude is never seen (a 53° orbit does not reach the Arctic). Cluster templates (T4, T5, T6, T7, T9, T10, T11) deliver instantaneous geolocation on every look; single-satellite templates deliver detection and Doppler/AOA fixes.

Choosing between the families

Singles (T1, T2, T3, T8, T12)

Every satellite is a revisit node, so revisit per euro is three times better than clusters. Geolocation is coarse on a single pass (Doppler/AOA, 5–20 km) and improves with repeated looks: fixed sites and slow ships converge to kilometre class within a day; fast or bursty emitters do not. The Unseenlabs model.

Clusters (T4, T5, T6, T7, T9, T10)

Three satellites per node buy instantaneous TDOA/FDOA fixes on single bursts with confidence ellipses — the product governments pay for. Revisit is that of the node count. Formation keeping, timing and inter-satellite data exchange add engineering. The HawkEye 360 and CERES model.

Mixed (T11 and variants)

A sun-synchronous node for global catalogue building and polar downlink plus mid-inclination nodes for regional density. Costs two spacecraft thermal/power designs or a compromise, but hedges the market: global data sells to NATO/EU, regional persistence to the Hellenic anchor. HawkEye flies SSO plus 40–46° planes for this reason.

The recommended path in this study is T2 → T4 → T7 → T9/T10 (one satellite that becomes the first member of a cluster, then clusters in more planes), with T8-style singles added later only if detection-revisit products outsell geolocation. Section 02's roadmap prices that path; the coverage simulator lets you build any variant and test it against your own point of interest.
