A passive-RF constellation in low Earth orbit, built from Greece

Everything needed to decide, design, sell and build a 100–150 kg SIGINT microsatellite that listens from UHF to X-band, geolocates emitters first alone and then as a TDOA/FDOA cluster, and turns what it hears into EW databases, signature catalogues and activity-pattern libraries. Three study areas, one component model, live simulators and a working mock of the end product.

The study

What this study concludes, in one paragraph

A 100–150 kg satellite is a generous platform for this mission: the core TALOS-1 payload closes at about 24 kg and 124 W, leaving room for a deployable reflector, an inter-satellite link and propellant for formation flying and a five-year disposal. Detection is not the hard part — radars are visible through their sidelobes from anywhere in the footprint and tactical UHF/L-band comms sit 15–25 dB above the noise — the hard parts are dynamic range (a surveillance radar's mainlobe arrives at −24 dBm next to −120 dBm comms), self-interference on a small bus, nanosecond timing for TDOA, and the data reduction that turns gigabits per second of IQ into kilobytes of reports. A single satellite sells monitoring and coarse geolocation (5–20 km per pass, 1–5 km over many passes); the first three-satellite cluster is what unlocks instantaneous fixes at hundreds of metres to a few kilometres, and that cluster — not the tenth satellite — is the commercial and funding threshold the plan is built around (€18–35 M demonstrator, €40–65 M cluster, 2030/2032).
