04 Service demo · Operations console

Operations console (demo)

A working mock of the end product: the web application a customer analyst or collection manager would use to see what TALOS has detected, where emitters are, when the next pass comes and what to task. Everything below runs in the browser from synthetic data generated at load time; the orbits, passes and contact windows are computed with the study's orbit engine for the baseline 530 km / 53° constellation.

Baseline: TALOS-1 v0.3Sources: research notes 03 §5, 04 §4–§8Data: synthetic, seeded, regenerated per constellation stage
The console is dark by design

Operations rooms run dark displays to keep map contrast high and to match the common operating pictures analysts already use (TAK, SitaWare, ArcGIS dashboards). This page forces the dark theme; the rest of the study follows your theme setting. The banners say UNCLASSIFIED // DEMO because every emitter, detection, alert and vessel identity here is generated by a seeded random process tied to the simulated satellite passes — none of it describes a real unit or site.

    How an analyst would use it

    The console follows the feature set that the public descriptions of HawkEye 360 Mission Space, Kleos Guardian LOCATE, the Kratos satID/Monics spectrum tools and military common operating pictures converge on: a layered map with confidence ellipses, a timeline with pass markers, a per-pass spectrum view, AOI tasking with pass prediction, an alert rule set and a catalogue browser with maturity levels[R4-12][R4-8][R4-34]. A typical morning for the three roles the research identifies looks like this[R4-29]:

    1. Collection manager opens Passes to see the day's contacts per ground station, then Tasking to check that the standing AOIs (Aegean, Levant, Black Sea) have passes with usable geometry in the next 24 h; new customer requests are drawn on the map, the predicted dwell and CEP class are read off before the task is accepted, and the capacity impact is checked against the roughly 70 collection-minutes per satellite per day that a Kleos-class cluster could deliver[R4-8].
    2. RF / ELINT analyst works the Alerts feed: a jammer activation near a port, a radar whose PRI mode changed, a dark vessel with its navigation radar on. Each alert links to the emitter, whose panel shows the parametric set, the geolocation history and the ellipse; the analyst can cue SAR/EO on a fix, as HawkEye 360 and Airbus did for the Galápagos fleet[R4-7], or add the emitter to a watchlist.
    3. EW database manager uses Catalogue to promote emitters along the maturity ladder (Candidate → Provisional → Confirmed → Characterised), exports the parametric observations that feed the national or NATO library, and watches the Spectrum strip to spot occupancy changes in a band before any single emitter is classified.

    Drag the timeline to replay the last 24 h: detections disappear when no satellite was overhead, which is the honest picture of what a one-satellite or one-cluster service delivers at mid-latitudes — a handful of passes a day, clustered into two groups, with gaps of ten hours or more in between[R4-17]. Switch the constellation stage to see how the picture fills in with three clusters and four planes.

    About this demo — what is simulated, and what the real system would do differently

    Simulated here

    • Orbits: circular 530 km / 53° orbits with J2 secular drift (ORB.makeOrbit), one satellite, a 3-satellite along-track cluster (150 km spacing, ±20 km cross-track), three clusters in three planes, or four clusters in four planes. Positions, footprints, ground tracks, passes over AOIs and ground-station contacts are all propagated live.
    • Emitters: about 270 synthetic instances placed along shipping lanes, on coasts, at plausible inland radar sites, in conflict regions and at teleport-class locations, with frequency, bandwidth, modulation, PRI/PW/scan and EIRP drawn from the ranges of the emitter classes in emitters.js.
    • Detections: generated only inside the computed visibility windows (elevation ≥ 10° at the emitter) of each cluster, with a class-dependent detection probability (continuous emitters almost always, bursty nets about 60 %, fire-control illuminators rarely). SNR is computed from the link budget at the pass geometry using sidelobe EIRP for radars.
    • Ellipses: a simple model — radars 0.2–1 km, jammers 0.3–1.5 km, communications 1–5 km semi-major axis for a cluster, ×3 for the single satellite, worsened at low elevation — not a full CRLB (that lives in the TDOA/FDOA simulator). Ellipses are drawn ×10 by default because kilometre-class ellipses are sub-pixel at regional zoom; switch to true scale in the Layers panel.
    • Alerts, maturity and confidence: rule-based on the synthetic detections (first detection in an AOI, jammer activation after a quiet period, flagged radar mode change, dark vessel with radar on).

    The real system

    • Orbit knowledge from GNSS-derived state vectors and precise orbit determination (metre-class), not Keplerian propagation; passes from the mission planning system with antenna masks and station schedules.
    • Detections from on-board channelisation, CFAR detection and PDW formation, downlinked as reports plus IQ snapshots, then cross-correlated on the ground (CAF) for TDOA/FDOA; see on-board processing and the software architecture.
    • Ellipses from the measurement covariance (timing, frequency, geometry, clock bias), terrain-adjusted; the predicted CEP shown in Tasking would come from the same estimator run on the planned geometry, as the research recommends[R4-16].
    • Platform association from AIS, ADS-B and customer ground truth; maturity transitions logged for audit; classification banners, release caveats and per-customer data rights enforced by the platform, not by a label.
    • Basemaps (nautical, aeronautical, imagery), APP-6 symbology, STANAG 4676 track export, OGC API Features and webhook delivery into the customer's own COP[R4-44].

    Design notes

    Classification and provenance banners are not decoration: MoD demos are refused without them, and the audit trail behind maturity transitions is what government customers ask for[R4-28]. Predicted CEP before tasking is the single most important honesty feature — the market quotes "few hundred metres to few kilometres" and the customer must see which conditions apply to their AOI, signal bandwidth and geometry[R4-29]. Time is the primary axis: with 2–4 useful passes a day per cluster the question "when was this true?" matters as much as "where?", so the scrubber sits under the map and every panel re-evaluates against the view time. Spectrum before emitters: occupancy per pass is a Stage 1 product that needs no geolocation, which is why the spectrum strip filters the map by frequency rather than the other way round[R4-13].

    Performance choices for the demo: the constellation's leader states are precomputed once per stage at 60 s steps over the 96 h synthetic window; emitter visibility is a dot product against those states; ground tracks are cached per minute of view time; the map redraws at 5 Hz only while the clock is playing. The whole page works from file:// with no network.
