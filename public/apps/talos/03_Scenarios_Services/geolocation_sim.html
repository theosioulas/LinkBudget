The physics in the model

Measurements

For satellites \(i, j\) at positions \(\mathbf r_i, \mathbf r_j\) with velocities \(\mathbf v_i, \mathbf v_j\) and an emitter at \(\mathbf x\) on the Earth's surface:

$$ \Delta\tau_{ij} = \frac{|\mathbf x-\mathbf r_i| - |\mathbf x-\mathbf r_j|}{c} \qquad \Delta f_{ij} = \frac{f_0}{c}\left(\dot r_i - \dot r_j\right),\; \dot r_i = \mathbf v_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}_i $$

Each TDOA is a hyperboloid, each FDOA an iso-Doppler surface; the map draws their zero-residual intersections with the Earth's surface for every satellite pair (reference satellite 1 against the others, the independent set).

Thermal-noise bounds (Stein form, as used by HawkEye 360[R3-2])

$$ \sigma_\tau = \frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{BT\gamma}},\; \beta = \frac{2\pi B}{\sqrt{12}} \qquad \sigma_f = \frac{1}{T_e\sqrt{BT\gamma}},\; T_e = \frac{2\pi T}{\sqrt{12}} $$ $$ \gamma = \left(\frac{1}{\gamma_1}+\frac{1}{\gamma_2}+\frac{1}{\gamma_1\gamma_2}\right)^{-1} $$

\(B\) is the signal bandwidth, \(T\) the integration time, \(\gamma\) the effective pair SNR built from the per-satellite SNRs \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) of the link budget \(P_r = EIRP - L_{fs}(d, f_0) + G_r - L\), \(N = kT_0B + NF\). Integrating \(n\) pulses or snapshots divides both bounds by \(\sqrt n\). The rectangular-spectrum assumption gives \(\beta \approx 1.81B\); real signals with energy at the band edges (chirps, PSK) do slightly better, smooth-spectrum FM slightly worse.

Error floors and covariance

$$ \sigma_{\tau}^2 = \sigma_{\tau,th}^2 + \sigma_{sync}^2 + \frac{2\sigma_{pos}^2}{c^2} \qquad \sigma_f^2 = \sigma_{f,th}^2 + \sigma_{LO}^2 + \frac{2 f_0^2\sigma_{vel}^2}{c^2} $$

The Jacobian \(\mathbf H\) of the measurements with respect to the emitter's east–north position is evaluated analytically (gradient of a range is the unit vector, gradient of a range rate is the velocity component normal to the line of sight divided by range). The least-squares covariance is

$$ \mathbf C = \left(\mathbf H^{\mathsf T}\mathbf W\mathbf H\right)^{-1},\; \mathbf W = \mathrm{diag}(1/\sigma_k^2) $$

A pair's TDOA gradient is its baseline projected across the line of sight: the component transverse to the ground line of sight counts in full, the component along it only as \(\sin^2\varepsilon\). The weak axis of a TDOA fix therefore points from the emitter towards the formation — cross-track when the emitter is abeam, along-track when it is ahead or behind. The eigen-decomposition of \(\mathbf C\) gives the 1σ semi-axes; the 95 % ellipse scales them by \(\sqrt{-2\ln(0.05)} = 2.45\) and the CEP is approximated by \(0.59(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)\). A useful rule of thumb for the geometry factor is \(\mathrm{GDOP}\approx d/L\): 1,000 km slant range over a 100 km baseline multiplies the range-difference error by about ten[R3-2][R3-12] — but only along the baseline. Perpendicular to it the relevant baseline is the formation's cross-track extent \(W\), so a TDOA-only fix from a 150 km × 20 km formation has a geometry factor near \(d/W \approx 50\) cross-track; FDOA, whose gradient is strongest across the track, removes most of that penalty, which is why the hybrid is the baseline product.

Single-satellite methods

$$ \sigma_\theta = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\lambda\,\sigma_\phi}{2\pi D\cos\theta}\right)^2 + \sigma_{att}^2},\quad \text{ground error} \approx d\,\sigma_\theta \;(\text{cross}),\; \frac{d\,\sigma_\theta}{\sin\varepsilon}\;(\text{along the look}) $$

For AOA the phase error combines the calibration residual with thermal noise \(1/\sqrt{BT\gamma}\). Doppler-only geolocation over a pass is shown qualitatively: position error \(\approx \lambda\,\sigma_f\,d/v\) times a geometry factor of 1–10, with a mirror solution across the track[R3-13][R3-14].

Model limits

Read the ellipses as lower bounds

The simulator computes the Cramér–Rao bound of an unbiased estimator, not the performance of a real cross-ambiguity-function processor; practical systems sit 1.5–3× above it at low SNR and lose the fix altogether below roughly 0 dB per satellite. Only thermal noise and the four stated floors are modelled: no multipath, no ionospheric or tropospheric differential delay (metres at L-band, tens of metres at UHF), no emitter motion during the snapshot, no emitter frequency drift, no clock drift within the integration time, no mutual interference. The emitter is constrained to the Earth's surface at zero altitude; the mirror ambiguity of two-satellite and collinear geometries is not represented in the ellipse. Orbits are circular with J2 drift; cross-track offsets are produced by RAAN differences, so the realised separation varies with latitude and is reported in the satellite table. The TDOA bound uses the Stein convention quoted in the research; the Knapp–Carter form in orbit.js (\(\sqrt{2BT\gamma}\)) is a factor √2 more optimistic — a bandwidth-definition convention, not a physical difference.
