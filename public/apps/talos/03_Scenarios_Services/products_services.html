Products, services & pricing

What TALOS sells, at which stage, in which contract form and at what price posture. The product ladder goes from detections to analytics with tasking and sovereign capacity alongside; the pricing patterns and anchors come from the contracts that exist in the market today; a revenue-scenario calculator lets you test subscription counts and anchor contracts against the opex of the three cost scenarios; and the go-to-market sequence, the talking points and the list of what we will not claim are written for the people who will sell this.

Product ladder

The ladder mirrors the L1–L4 levels of the data pipeline: each step adds processing, fusion and analyst work, each step has a higher margin, and each step becomes credible at a later constellation stage. Two offers sit beside the ladder rather than on it: IQ dedicated tasking, which sells minutes and raw snapshots to customers who have their own exploitation, and sovereign capacity, which sells a committed share of a cluster with in-country processing — the two forms HawkEye 360 describes as IQ tasking and dedicated capacity, with Clusters 15/16 the first built around a committed-capacity customer [R4-2][R1-5].

Kleos's price structure — the clearest public reference

Kleos's February 2023 brochure is the only public statement of how a space-RF service is structured and priced: Guardian LOCATE as data-as-a-service (frequency, time, position, confidence ellipse per detection over an encrypted REST API) on a monthly or annual subscription priced by AOI with daily revisit; and Mission-as-a-Service on a four-satellite cluster offering up to 9 min per orbit (~70 min/day) of collection per satellite, with price a function of the percentage of capacity, taskability and exclusivity, options for direct-to-user downlink and on-premises processing, and unrestricted data rights for exclusive tasking [R4-8]. Kleos's bankruptcy five months later is the other half of the reference: the structure was right, the anchor customer and the analytics layer were missing [R1-31]. TALOS keeps the structure and reverses the order: anchor first.

Competitive positioning

Three operators define the commercial market; TALOS does not try to beat them on scale. Its position is the EU-sourced, sovereign-processable, NATO-format-native regional system with a published accuracy model, in a region where the incumbents sell from Luxembourg, Rennes and Virginia.

Go-to-market sequence

The sequence is ordered by the procurement clocks of the buyers (research 04 §3): EU bodies move on RFIs and calls, the Hellenic MoD can sign an LOI before hardware exists, NATO buys services once they are demonstrated, and commercial maritime partners buy a layer once it is on an API. It is the inverse of the Kleos order (commercial data first, anchor never).

  • 2027 Q1–Q3 · Phase 0/A
    EUSPA engagement and RFI follow-up

    Position TALOS against the space-based GNSS RFI monitoring RFI (Part A service from 2030, Part B hosted-payload option earlier); answer the RFI's questions on update rate, time-to-localise-and-notify and single- vs multi-satellite accuracy with the models on this site [R4-16]. Deliverable: a service concept note and a letter of interest.

  • 2027 Q2–2028 · Phase A/B
    EDF consortium

    Join or form a consortium for an EDF SPACE-ISR-type call (SIGINT eligible, EU/associated-state consortia, CONOPS through prototyping funded) [R4-27]; partners: a European bus vendor, a Greek AIT house, an analytics SME, a national research centre. Deliverable: funded pathfinder work and the first EU validation of the catalogue design.

  • 2027 Q3 → 2029 · G0 → G2
    Hellenic MoD anchor

    LOI at G0 (a gate condition), then a milestone-paid anchor contract covering demonstrator products (regional monitoring, EW database seeding) and an option on sovereign cluster capacity; joint tasking board with defence as recommended for the national space posture [R4-25]. Hellas Space 2.0 has no RF line yet — the pitch is the missing modality [R4-24].

  • 2030–2031 · after IOC
    NATO APSS / Aquila contribution

    Offer TALOS-1 products as a Greek national contribution to the Alliance Persistent Surveillance from Space virtual constellation and respond to NCIA's commercial ISR data market opportunity; STANAG 4676/4559 native output is the entry ticket [R4-26][R4-36].

  • 2031 → 2033 · Stage 2
    Commercial maritime and aviation partners

    Source-layer licensing to Windward/Kpler-class platforms, P&I clubs and insurers for GNSS-interference and dark-vessel layers (the Windward jamming features prove the demand [R4-19]); EMSA and Frontex EUROSUR layers; interference-hunting service for GEO operators and EETT. Government-to-government offers (Cyprus, Gulf) once the cluster flies.

  • 2034 →
    Analytics and persistence tiers

    Luno-style analytics for NGA-type buyers, persistence SLAs at Stage 4, second sovereign customer. Revenue mix target: government ≥ 60% (every surviving operator is government-anchored [R1-20]), commercial the rest.

Sales talking points

  • Regional persistence from the first satellite. A 53° orbit gives the Aegean, the Black Sea approaches and the Levant 4–7 passes a day from one spacecraft — computed, not claimed — where global constellations spread their minutes across the planet.
  • Radars and jammers are day-one products. They are never sensitivity-limited from LEO; the catalogue of S/X-band naval and coastal radars and of L-band jammers starts filling during commissioning.
  • Predicted accuracy before you pay. The tasking interface shows the passes and the CEP for your emitter class and geometry before accepting the task; no competitor publishes an accuracy model.
  • EU-sourced and sovereign-processable. ITAR-free baseline, EU sovereign cloud, an accredited national enclave, and a catalogue designed so a classified annex attaches to an unclassified core.
  • NATO formats native. STANAG 4676 tracks and a 4559-compatible library export from the start — a gap in every commercial offer today [R4-44].
  • A living EW database, not a data feed. The product is the emitter catalogue with maturity levels, parametric history and pattern-of-life baselines; the detections are its raw material.
  • Cueing, not replacing. RF narrows the search box for SAR, EO and maritime patrol; the Galápagos and Galwan cases are the pattern, with ICEYE Hellas and the national EO microsats as the local partners.
  • The 1 → 3 step is where the value is. Independent CYGNSS data: median detection latency 3 h 46 min with one satellite, 48 min with seven, most of the gain early [R4-17]. Buy the demonstrator products now, option the cluster.

What we will not claim

The honesty list — every item has cost a competitor credibility or is below the physics
  • Detection of individual consumer drones from orbit. DJI-class links at 26–33 dBm EIRP are 9–16 dB below the thermal floor even at nadir with any aperture that fits a 150 kg bus. We sell UAS activity: ground control stations and military-class links.
  • Detection of personal privacy devices (10 mW GNSS jammers). SNR −46 dB. We map jammers above roughly 10 W and all military-class jammers; we do not map every event an ADS-B degradation map shows.
  • Content. No demodulation of voice or data, no transcription, no message content, encrypted or not. Externals only: who, where, when, what signature. (Spire's voice transcription is a state-mandate product; it is not ours.)
  • A numeric accuracy without conditions. Every CEP we quote carries its signal bandwidth, duration, SNR and geometry; a "500 m" headline without them is marketing.
  • Global coverage or sub-hour revisit before Stage 4. One satellite is 4–7 regional passes a day with 10–14 h gaps; the cluster does not change the pass count, only the accuracy.
  • Tactical latency before the inter-satellite link. Standard tier is hours, NRT is under 90 minutes with contracted complements; minutes come with Stage 3.
  • Detecting a silent target. EMCON defeats passive RF; we report non-detections as non-detections and fuse with SAR, AIS gaps and timing.
  • C/X-band VSAT hunting as a continuous survey. Sidelobe SNR ≈ 0 dB with the baseline aperture; it is a tasked investigation with the reflector option, long dwell or a cue.
