EW database & signature catalogue

The catalogue is the product; detections are its raw material. This page sets out what national EW libraries contain and why a commercial catalogue sits one level above them, then specifies the TALOS data model (seven entities, rendered as an entity-relationship diagram), the maturity levels and their transition rules, the de-confliction and change-detection logic, the pattern-of-life analytics, the classified annex, the one-way flow into national libraries, example records, export formats and governance.

What national EW libraries contain

The US Electronic Warfare Integrated Reprogramming Database (EWIRDB), managed by DIA, is "the primary DoD-approved source for technical parametric and performance data" on non-communications emitters. It merges NSA's KILTING national technical ELINT parametric database, multi-source scientific and technical assessments of foreign emitters, and US emitter data from the service EW support agencies; fourteen offices at ten organisations contribute and updates were historically released quarterly [R4-28]. NATO maintains the analogous NATO Emitter Database (NEDB, also referred to as the EW database, EWDB) fed by nations (general knowledge). These libraries are what RWR/ESM mission libraries are built from: a radar warning receiver that says "SA-20 Big Bird" is reading a record whose lineage goes back to KILTING.

What a commercial space-RF catalogue contains

HawkEye 360 describes a "growing unclassified emitter database" built from years of unclassified collection, with "over one billion data points" used to train and validate algorithms [R4-1][R4-2]. Its signal-of-interest library lists signal classes — GMRS/FRS push-to-talk, VHF marine, EPIRB, cellular, satphone, X-band radar, Ku VSAT, GNSS jammers [R4-30]. The commercial catalogue therefore sits one level above the national library: it is a library of signal classes and of geolocated emitter instances with history, not a validated parametric ELINT database with ELNOTs and unit associations. That is exactly what makes it exportable and unclassified, and it is the design principle of the TALOS core.

The TALOS data model

Seven entities, one per processing level plus the two that span levels. Keys and the most important fields are shown; every entity carries created_at, updated_at, classification, release_caveat and audit_ref. The model follows the synthesis in the use-case research (04 §5.3) and the field lists that Kleos (frequency, time, lat/lon/ECEF, confidence ellipse) and HawkEye (frequency, bandwidth, modulation hints, duty cycle, confidence-bounded location) publish for their products [R4-8][R4-29].

Maturity levels

Maturity is the catalogue's statement of how sure it is that an emitter instance exists, where it is and what it is. The five levels are adapted from EW-library practice (04 §5.3); the transition rules are explicit so that a government customer can audit why a record says what it says, and so that the alert engine can weight records by maturity.

TransitionRuleActor
(new) → CandidateA detection does not gate to any existing instance (see de-confliction) and matches ≥1 signal class above the class's confidence thresholdAutomatic
Candidate → Provisional≥3 independent geolocations (different passes, or different satellites in a pass) consistent within their 95% ellipses, or for mobile emitters ≥3 track-consistent fixes; class unchangedAutomatic, analyst notified
Provisional → ConfirmedCorroboration by an independent source: SAR/EO detection inside the ellipse at a consistent time, AIS/ADS-B identity match, ground truth from the customer, or a known-site databaseAnalyst confirms
Confirmed → CharacterisedStable parametric set over ≥N observations (N per class, e.g. 10 for radars), modes catalogued, platform class assigned; eligible for the on-board SOI library and for export to national librariesEW database manager
any → Dormant flagNot observed on M consecutive observation opportunities (passes where the cell was collected in the right band); M per class (e.g. 20 for fixed radars, 5 for a jammer that was continuous)Automatic
any → ArchivedDormant beyond the retention threshold, or merged into another instance (duplicate), or customer-requested retirement; archived records remain queryable with their audit trailEW database manager
Provisional/Confirmed → Candidate (demote)Parametric distance to a new detection exceeds the class tolerance and no mode explains it (possible different emitter at the same site), or geolocation inconsistency beyond 3σ on ≥2 passesAutomatic, analyst review

De-confliction and change detection

Every new detection is gated against the catalogue before it can create a record. The logic below is the one the GOES hunt illustrates: a 1.9 s, 1.5 kHz emitter was isolated from co-channel neighbours by repeated collections, because its position and burst signature were consistent with each other and inconsistent with everything already known [R4-31]. "Mode change" and "dormant" are events the alert engine subscribes to.

function gate(detection d, geolocation g | null):
    cands = catalogue.query(class = d.class_id,
                            position within k·ellipse(g) if g else footprint(d),
                            maturity ≠ ARCHIVED)
    if g is null and d.aoa is null:                 # Stage 1, no fix yet
        cands = cands.filter(e => parametric_distance(e, d) < τ_class)
    for e in cands ranked by score(e, d):
        # score = w_pos · mahalanobis(g, e.best_position) + w_par · parametric_distance(e, d)
        if mahalanobis(g, e.best_position) ≤ k and parametric_distance(e, d) ≤ τ_class:
            attach(d, g, e); update_position(e, g); update_drift(e, d)
            if drift(e) > τ_mode and not explained_by(e.modes, d):
                emit MODE_CHANGE(e, d)              # new PRI, PW, scan or frequency set
                if e.maturity ≥ CONFIRMED: flag ANALYST_REVIEW
            return e
        if mahalanobis(g, e.best_position) ≤ k and parametric_distance(e, d) > τ_split:
            emit CO_LOCATED_CANDIDATE(e, d)         # a second emitter at the same site
            break
    e_new = create EmitterInstance(class = d.class_id, maturity = CANDIDATE, pos = g)
    attach(d, g, e_new)
    if quiet_baseline(aoi(d), d.class_id, cell(g)): emit NEW_EMITTER_IN_QUIET_AREA(e_new)
    return e_new

every observation_opportunity(aoi, band, pass):
    for e in catalogue.instances(aoi, band, maturity ≥ PROVISIONAL):
        if not observed(e, pass): e.miss_count += 1 else e.miss_count = 0
        if e.miss_count ≥ M_class(e): emit DORMANT(e); e.dormant_since = pass.t
        if e.dormant_since and observed(e, pass): emit REACTIVATED(e)  # jammer back on

Parametric distance is a per-class weighted norm over the fields that are stable for that class (for radars: frequency set, PRI family and values, pulse width, scan period; for comms: centre frequency, bandwidth, modulation class, burst length statistics). k is the gating scale on the error ellipse (typically 3); τ_class, τ_mode and τ_split are class tolerances learned in commissioning from calibration emitters. An observation opportunity is a pass during which the cell was actually collected in the relevant band, which is why the minutes ledger must be written back to the catalogue.

Pattern-of-life and anomaly analytics

Baselines are built per AOI, signal class, spatial cell and hour of week, and departures are flagged — a new radar on a previously quiet island, a GNSS jammer activating near a port, a surge in UHF push-to-talk in a training area — the way HawkEye 360 flagged jamming north of Chernobyl between November 2021 and February 2022 [R4-6] and RF activity in the Galwan Valley in 2020 [R4-14]. The detector is a z-score against a rolling quiet baseline with streak confirmation:

$$ z_{c,h} = \frac{x_{c,h} - \mu_{c,h}}{\max(\sigma_{c,h}, \sigma_{\min})} $$

with an alert raised when \( z_{c,h} \ge z_0 \) on \( k \) consecutive observation opportunities, where \(x_{c,h}\) is the activity measure in cell \(c\) at hour-of-week \(h\) (detection count, occupied bandwidth, or interference power), \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) are the rolling eight-week baseline statistics computed only over passes that actually covered the cell, and \(\sigma_{\min}\) prevents a perfectly quiet cell from alarming on a single weak detection. The independent CYGNSS GNSS-interference study used this construction and reports detector reliability rising from 86% with one satellite to 100% with three, while explicitly deferring the false-alarm characterisation [R4-17]; government customers will ask for both, so the calculator below shows what the threshold and the streak length do to the expected false-alert load under a Gaussian baseline — a lower bound on real false alarms, since real activity has heavier tails.

Classified annex design

EU buyers will expect classified handling and National Security Authority clearances [R4-16], and a Greek operator will need a legal basis and coordination with the Hellenic MoD and the National Intelligence Service for collection over foreign territory (04 §3.6). The annex is how the same catalogue serves both lines without ever moving classified fields into the exportable core.

Core (unclassified, exportable)

  • All seven entities with the fields in the ER diagram, minus the red lines
  • Platform association at class level only ("frigate-class S-band surveillance radar"), MMSI/ICAO24 when derived from public AIS/ADS-B
  • Parametric values at the resolution needed for the product (e.g. PRI to 0.1 µs), not at the full measurement resolution
  • Hosted in the EU sovereign cloud; NIS2 controls; EULA and export licence check on IQ and library exports

Annex (classified, national)

  • Tables keyed by the same class_id and emitter_id: ELNOT/ENOT mapping, specific unit and site association, national assessments, full-resolution parametric exemplars and IQ pointers
  • Sovereign customer's tasking records and alert rules
  • Hosted in the accredited enclave (NATO SECRET / EU SECRET target); one-way import of core updates through a data diode; no reverse path
  • Edits by cleared staff only, four-eyes rule, audit-logged; the annex can be handed to the customer in full at contract end

Feeding national libraries

Commercial geolocations and parametric observations can be contributed to NATO and national EW databases as low-classification source records; the reverse flow — library to commercial — is normally blocked (04 §5.3). The export is a packaged set of Characterised and Confirmed records in a 4559-compatible structure with source grading, delivered on a schedule or on request, and it never contains a national library's own content.

Governance

  • Audit log. Every create/update/transition/export on every entity writes an immutable audit record (actor, algorithm version or analyst id, evidence ids, before/after hash). Government customers expect to audit why a record says what it says; the maturity rules make that question answerable.
  • Release caveats. Each record carries a caveat (COMMERCIAL-EULA, CUSTOMER-EXCLUSIVE-<id>, GOV-ONLY, NATIONAL) that the API enforces per customer; exclusive tasking results are invisible to other subscribers until the exclusivity window expires.
  • Classification banners. Every view, export and report shows the classification of its most sensitive record; the core cannot render annex fields because it does not hold them.
  • Retention. Detections and snapshots: 24 months online, archive thereafter; instances: indefinite with archive state; customer-exclusive data: per contract.
  • Data protection. Where a communications emitter could be tied to an individual (satphone, PMR), the core stores activity statistics and class-level records, not persistent per-device identifiers; the GDPR analysis in the legal gate (G0) defines the limits.
  • Versioning. Baselines, solver versions, classifier versions and SOI library uploads are versioned and referenced from the records they affect, so a re-processing can be reproduced.
  • Quality metrics. Per class: detection probability and false-alarm rate from calibration campaigns (a KPI customers ask for and nobody publishes), geolocation residual statistics against known sites, maturity-transition counts per month.
