Closing the Link: From Terrestrial Emitter to Real-Time Intelligence
Closing an orbital passive RF link does not require brute-force high-power transmitters. It is a precise physical sequence of wideband electromagnetic capture, ultra-low-noise amplification, and onboard edge decoupling that discards empty noise and downlinks pure pulse intelligence in seconds.
The Core Engineering Breakthrough

Why Most Systems Fail vs. How This Architecture Closes the Link

Digitizing 3.2 GHz of direct RF spectrum generates over 30 Gigabits per second of raw data. Streaming this volume directly to Earth is physically impossible on a 100–150 kg smallsat. How we close the link: The spacecraft carries an onboard FPGA edge processor (Innoflight CFC-400) running a 1024-channel Polyphase Filter Bank. It discards 99.9% of empty dead space in orbit and extracts only compact Pulse Descriptor Words (PDWs). Millions of radio pulses condense into a lightweight megabyte stream that beams down to ground stations or crosslinks across the constellation via laser in under 15 seconds.

Step 01 • Transmission
Ground Emitter Radiates
A naval radar, marine AIS transponder, battlefield GPS jammer, or tactical drone ground station broadcasts radio frequency energy into the sky. The signal disperses through 550+ km of atmosphere, arriving at orbit as a faint whisper.
Physics: Arriving power density is extremely low (10−14 to 10−11 W), requiring quiet receivers.
Step 02 • Intercept
Aperture Collection at 7.6 km/s
The satellite sweeps across an 800–1,200 km horizon. Deployable wideband antennas intercept the electromagnetic wavefront: a 1.85m deployable LPDA for UHF/VHF, a 4-spiral cavity array for S-band, and parabolic reflectors for C/X-band.
Kinematics: Deployable tape-spring booms fit a 1.85m antenna into a compact 3U stowage box.
Step 03 • Conditioning
Analog Front-End Protection & LNA
A diode limiter acts as an electronic circuit breaker, clamping high-power pulses from megawatt radars to prevent burnout. A low-noise GaAs amplifier (NF ≤ 1.2 dB) boosts the micro-signal cleanly, while switchable filters reject cell tower clutter.
Protection: Survives direct high-power mainlobe illuminations up to +30 dBm without damage.
Step 04 • Digitization
Direct RF Sampling (3.2 GSPS)
Instead of heavy, drifting analog frequency downconverters, a space-qualified 12-bit ADC samples the incoming electromagnetic wave directly into digital I/Q streams. The physical radio wave becomes mathematical bits in nanoseconds.
Simplicity: Replaces 8 analog mixers, local oscillators, and IF stages with a single digital chip.
Step 05 • Edge Decoupling
Onboard PDW Pulse Extraction
The FPGA Polyphase Filter Bank (PFB) slices the spectrum into narrow channels. A CFAR threshold detector extracts exact Pulse Descriptor Words: Time-of-Arrival (TOA), Pulse Width (PW), Carrier Frequency, Amplitude, and Bearing.
Compression: Squeezes 30 Gbps raw RF noise into a lightweight < 500 kbps metadata stream.
Step 06 • Delivery
Instant Downlink & EOB Matching
Extracted pulse metadata transmits effortlessly over a standard X-band radio link or routes across optical laser crosslinks (O-ISL) in under 15 seconds. Ground stations match pulse signatures against Electronic Order of Battle threat catalogues.
Result: Automatically names the exact ship, radar model, or jammer coordinates in real time.
Payload Signal Routing: Do All Antennas Go into a Single SDR?
No single SDR port connects directly to all antennas. The payload utilizes an intermediate Radio Frequency Front-End (RFFE) Conditioning & Switch Matrix to protect digitizers, filter out-of-band clutter, and route signals into 4 phase-coherent receiver channels.
Stage A • Apertures (Deck 1)
Multiple Antennas for Different Tasks
Different missions require different antennas: A 4-Spiral Cavity Array provides wide-angle Direction Finding (AOA), a Deployable Helical/LPDA surveys wideband UHF/L/S-band spectrum, and a Deployable Parabolic Mesh Dish delivers high gain for narrow-beam C/X-band pulse extraction.
Physics: Each antenna has distinct beamwidth, polarization, and frequency range.
Stage B • RFFE & Switch Matrix
Protection & Dynamic Routing
Signals pass through PIN diode limiters (preventing burnout from 100 kW radars), band-tuned LNAs, and a 5-band switched filter bank. A high-speed solid-state RF switch matrix dynamically steers the active antenna feeds to the SDR channels in < 50 ns.
Protection: Clamps high-power radar illuminations up to +30 dBm CW before they can reach the SDR.
Stage C • Coherent SDR & FPGA (Deck 2)
4-Channel Coherent Receiver
The SDR provides 4 phase-coherent receiver channels sharing a single atomic clock reference:
• AOA Mode: Simultaneously digitizes all 4 spiral antennas to compute nanosecond phase delays (Δφ) for instant line-of-bearing.
• High-Gain Mode: Routes high-gain parabolic/helical feeds for deep pulse demodulation.
Coherence: 4 synchronized ADCs sample in lockstep, enabling instant Direction-Finding without moving parts.

Can a Single Satellite Get Geolocation & Signal Intelligence?
Yes. A single LEO satellite can perform autonomous spectrum cataloguing and generate independent ground fixes using dual-axis phase interferometry combined with Doppler rate-of-change analysis.
Single-Sat Method 1 • Bearing
4-Spiral Phase Interferometry (AOA)
The satellite mounts 4 cavity-backed spiral antennas in an orthogonal cross on its Earth-facing deck (d = 0.55 m baseline). When a radar pulse arrives, it hits antenna 1 slightly before antenna 2. By measuring the sub-nanosecond phase difference, the satellite computes an instantaneous Line of Bearing (cone of arrival) directly to the target emitter.
Result: Instantaneous bearing measurement (≤ 1.2° accuracy) on every single pulse intercepted.
Single-Sat Method 2 • Ranging
Doppler Rate-of-Change (df/dt)
Moving at 7.6 km/s, the satellite observes a changing frequency shift as it passes an emitter (the Doppler S-curve). At the exact instant of closest approach (TCA), the rate of frequency change (ḟ) reaches maximum steepness. This rate mathematically defines the perpendicular cross-track range to the emitter: Rmin = f0v2 / (c·|ḟ|).
Ambiguity Removal: Fusing Doppler ranging with the 4-spiral Phase Interferometer immediately eliminates left/right mirror ambiguity on the very first pulse burst.
Single-Satellite Performance & Operational Floor 1 Satellite Baseline

• Geolocation Fix Accuracy: Produces a ground error ellipse of 10 to 50 km CEP on a single 3-to-5 minute orbital overpass.
• Operational Utility: Highly effective for regional threat assessment, verifying whether a radar or jammer is active in a military district, and cataloguing electronic signatures.
• Operational Limit: Revisit cadence is 6 to 12 hours between passes over a given region, and it cannot geolocate short (< 1 sec) frequency-hopping bursts with high precision.

Outcome: Classifies radar weapon type and deconflicts flight strike corridors in real time.
